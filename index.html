About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Public Administration at the School of Public Affairs and Administration, Rutgers University–Newark, where I expect to complete my degree in spring 2027. I study public management, focusing on digital governance, accountability, and social equity. My research examines the consequences of governments' use of AI and other technologies, and how they can be governed to produce effective, accountable, and equitable outcomes.

My dissertation, The Contingent Effects of Artificial Intelligence on Public Management: Evidence Across Bureaucrats, Public Organizations, and Citizens, reframes the question of government AI from whether it works or harms to the conditions under which it can be beneficial, equitable, and legitimate. Across three chapters, I examine these conditions at three levels. Within agencies, I find that organizational culture, supervisor support, and AI system features shape public employees' experience of AI. In the surrounding institutional environment, I show that administrative centralization conditions AI's effects on local service performance across racial contexts. At the interface between bureaucracy and the public, I demonstrate that workforce representation helps governments retain legitimacy when they deploy AI. Drawing on quasi-experimental, experimental, and observational designs and grounded in institutional and organizational theories, my dissertation advances a context-grounded account of how social, organizational, and institutional settings shape the effects of government AI.
